We are pleased to submit our original research manuscript entitled "A Mechanistic Study of Scale-Dependent Optimization Stagnation in C1 Piecewise-Quadratic Rectifiers Under Class Imbalance: Bias-Offset Equivalence, Layer-Wise Gradient Dynamics, and Scale-Adaptive Remedies" by Preecha Noiumkar and Thawatchai Chomsiri for consideration for publication in Intelligent Systems with Applications.

1. Fit with Intelligent Systems with Applications:
Deep convolutional neural networks deployed in intelligent rare-event screening systems (such as genomic variant screening in high-throughput sequencing, visual defect/digit anomaly detection, and highly imbalanced tabular decision systems) frequently operate under severe class imbalance (10:1 to 200:1) and varying input signal scales. While C1 piecewise-quadratic rectifiers (such as SmeLU, Smooth RectLU, and trainable AS-RectLU) replace ReLU's non-differentiable kink using fast elementary arithmetic, we show that anchoring their parabolic transition asymmetrically at the origin (L = 0, W = 2) introduces an implicit negative bias offset (Delta b = -1) and a vanishing origin derivative (f'(0+) = 0) that causes catastrophic multi-layer gradient stagnation when input amplitudes are small relative to transition width (sigma_l / W << 1).

2. Key Methodological and Practical Contributions:
- Exact Algebraic Identity & Causal Second-Moment Mechanics: We prove the exact bias-shift equivalence f_S(x; L, W) = SmeLU_{W/2}(x - L - W/2), derive layer-wise pre-activation (q_l) and post-activation (v_l) variance recurrences explaining 9-order-of-magnitude variance contraction, and confirm via direct Epoch-0 causal variance clamping (q_4 = 0.55 and 3.70) that eliminating fourth-layer variance collapse immediately restores first-layer weight gradients by 7 to 8 orders of magnitude and rescues seed convergence from 0/10 to 10/10 seeds.
- Fused Scale-Adaptive Remedy & Measured Computational Efficiency: We formulate Symmetric Per-Sample sigma-AS-RectLU as a single fused piecewise-quadratic operator embedding per-sample RMS scale adaptation. Controlled six-way all-layer ablations on 50 x 101 x 4 NIST GIAB HG001 read-pileup tensors confirm that fused Symmetric sigma-AS-RectLU matches explicit 3-step modular RMSNorm + SmeLU + inverse rescaling in accuracy (MCC(0.50) = 0.9700 vs. 0.9691; AUROC = 0.9999) while reducing measured training latency by 11.9% (959.78 ms/epoch vs. 1089.53 ms/epoch) and outperforming all-layer LayerNorm + SmeLU in both speed (-33.6%) and stability (MCC = 0.9700 vs. 0.7537).
- Rigorous Empirical Validation Without Resampling Approximations: Across 2x2 factorial experiments on synthetic 1D/2D motifs, Clean/Noisy MNIST (100:1), Imbalanced Tabular data (200:1), and both variant-scrubbed and unscrubbed natural 2D NIST GIAB HG001 read-pileup benchmarks, all claims are supported by direct empirical measurements across N = 10 to N = 30 paired random seeds (30/30 paired seed wins on low-scale 2D GIAB), K = 6 disjoint contiguous genomic test sub-blocks (180 block evaluations), and systematic learning-rate, gradient-clipping, and prior-bias ablations across Adam, RMSprop, and momentum SGD.

All datasets, per-seed confusion logs (.csv), and PyTorch verification scripts are publicly archived at https://github.com/preecha-msu/RectLU-Genomic-Collapse.
